Settings most sections share

A handful of settings come back in almost every section. They work the same way everywhere, so they are explained once here.

Color scheme

Picks one of the schemes defined in Theme settings, Colors. The scheme paints the section background, its text and its buttons.

Section background

Most sections can paint their own background on top of the color scheme. Background mode offers five choices, and the settings of the mode you pick appear right below it:

  • None (use the color scheme): the default. The scheme background shows, like everywhere else.
  • Solid color: one Background color.
  • Gradient (two colors): Gradient start, Gradient end and Gradient angle.
  • Image: a Background image with an optional Overlay color and Overlay opacity to keep text readable.
  • Radial halo: a Halo color glowing from one corner over a Base color, with Halo position, Halo size (desktop), Halo size (mobile) and Halo solid area.

A gradient takes a Gradient style: Linear, Radial glow (a halo of the lighter color, placed and sized by the halo settings) or Aurora (soft fields of the two colors, which Theme settings, Backgrounds can set in slow motion). An image takes an optional Background image (mobile), used under 768 px, and each picture shows at its focal point. Grain lays a light texture over any mode; 0 turns it off.

The colors and the image can be connected to shop metafields, so one value restyles every section at once. The Fallback background color is used when a connected metafield is empty; leave it on None to fall back to the color scheme, and never connect it to a metafield itself. The keys to create are listed on Metafields. On the product page the same settings connect to product metafields instead, so every product can carry its own background. The Product section also has a sixth mode, Product gradient (metafields), which reads each product's colors with nothing to connect: see Product gradient.

Spacing, desktop and mobile

Sizes and spacings come in pairs: a label ending in (desktop) applies from 768 px wide, one ending in (mobile) applies below. Top and bottom padding set the space inside the section, above and below its content.

Text sizes are the exception: they are fluid. The (mobile) value applies on a screen 390 px wide, the (desktop) value from 1440 px, and the size moves smoothly in between, then Heading scale or Body scale multiplies it. See Theme settings, Typography. The announcement bar and the mega menu switch their text sizes at 1200 px instead, and decorative texts, such as the text behind cutouts of the Product section, keep a desktop and a mobile size off the scale.

Section width

Almost every section has a Section width setting, right after its color scheme. It sets how far the section's background, image or card reaches:

  • Full width: from one edge of the screen to the other. Pictures and videos (Image, Image with text, Before and after, Video, Slideshow) run edge to edge; text sections keep their text to a readable width inside.
  • Full width with margins: the whole screen less the side margins of the header. The section's edges line up with the header's at every width, and with the floating header card when the header floats.
  • Page width: stops at the Page width of Theme settings, Layout, with its side margin, so the section lines up with the product, collection and search pages.

The setting replaces the width settings that some sections used to have on their own (Before and after, Image, Image with text, Lookbook, Custom Liquid, Slideshow, Video and Apps).

The header over a section

With Header behavior set to Transparent at top (the default) or Adaptive in the Header section, the header can lie over the first section of a page, transparent, in the colors that suit what is under it. See Automatic header colors.

  • Hero, Slideshow, Video, Products showcase and Brand story hero carry Allow transparent header. When the section is first on the page, the header lies over it and starts transparent. The extra top padding settings keep your content clear of the header only while it is transparent.
  • Many other sections carry Header over this section, off by default: Contact, FAQ, Image, Page, Rich text, Multicolumn, Routine, Key ingredients, Key figures, Bento, Scroll-revealed text, Featured product, Before and after, Collection list, Compare products, Custom Liquid, Logo list, Blog, Blog post, 404, List of collections and Collection. Checked on the first section of the template, the section's background (color, gradient or image) runs up behind the announcement bar and the header, and its content starts under the header.
  • The Product section has neither. In its Product gradient background mode, Extend the background under the header plays that part: see Extend the gradient under the header.

With Header behavior on Standard, these settings have no effect: the header keeps its card above the page.

Text colors

Most recent sections have a Text colors setting. Color scheme keeps the text of the section's scheme. Auto reads the real background of the section (its color, gradient or picture) and keeps the scheme's text when it reaches a contrast of 4.5:1 there, otherwise switches to white or black, for the text, icons, lines and buttons. Pick Auto when a section background makes the scheme's text hard to read.

Inner panel and text color

The About page sections and Contact place their content on an inner panel, the layer between the background and the text: Solid uses the color and opacity you set, Glass uses the shared glass recipe, Hidden removes it. Text color left on None lets the section work out a readable color from the background and the panel; pick a color only to force one.

Heading level

Search engines and screen readers expect exactly one main heading (H1) per page. Sections that often open a page (Hero, FAQ, Contact, Featured set, Store locator, Brand story hero) let you choose Main heading (H1) or a subheading level. The templates that open with one of them already set H1; keep H2 anywhere else. Product, collection, blog and page templates set their own H1.

Text left empty

Many text settings say Leave empty to use the translated default. Left empty, they show a built in text that is already translated into every language the theme ships (English, French, German, Italian and Spanish). Typing your own text replaces it in every language, so on a multilingual store either leave these fields empty or translate your text with the Translate & Adapt app. See Translations.

Spam protection notice

Shopify protects the contact and newsletter forms with hCaptcha, which must be disclosed. Balm prints the required sentence, with its two links, under each form, which removes the hCaptcha badge from the corner of the page. The Spam protection notice settings only change its size, color and alignment.

Header and footer

These sections live in the header and footer groups, shared by every page of the store.

Announcement bar

WhereHeader group only.

A thin bar above the header, made of Message blocks. With two messages or more they rotate on their own, with optional previous and next arrows, and each message can link somewhere. With Show close button on, visitors can dismiss the bar for the rest of their visit.

Scroll behavior keeps the bar pinned, lets it scroll away with the page, or hides it on the way down and brings it back on the way up. Bar layout set to Floating turns it into a card that matches the floating header, and Bar style set to Glass uses the shared glass recipe.

A floating bar sits over the first section when the header is transparent there. Use the header's colors over dark backgrounds, off by default, then gives it the header's Colors over dark backgrounds whenever the header wears them: at rest over a dark first section, and over every dark section with Adaptive. It returns to its own color scheme together with the header. Content color set to Auto keeps the text, the link and the icons readable on the bar's own color, or on what shows through its glass. See Automatic header colors.

Typical use. Two or three short messages that rotate: a free shipping threshold, a returns promise and a launch announcement that links to the new product.

Header

WhereHeader group only.

The logo, the main menu, predictive search, the account and cart icons, country and language selectors, and on desktop the icons of your social media profiles (Show social media icons, which uses the links of Theme settings, Social media). The logo is an image, or a text wordmark set in the heading font. On phones the menu opens as a sheet under the header or as a full height drawer.

Header behavior sets how the header sits on the page:

  • Standard: the header always shows its card, in its color scheme.
  • Transparent at top, the default: over a first section that allows it (see The header over a section), the header is transparent, in the colors that suit that section, then takes its card on scroll.
  • Adaptive: the header stays transparent or glass at every scroll position, on every page, and its colors and logo follow the section under it. Background when scrolled picks Glass or Transparent.

Card background sets the card of Standard, and of Transparent at top once the page scrolls: Solid, in the header color scheme, or Glass. Floating header (detached) pulls the header away from the screen edges as a rounded card, with its own top margin, side margins and corner radius on desktop and on mobile.

Automatic header colors

With Transparent at top or Adaptive, the header works out on its own whether what lies under it is light or dark, with nothing to set per section. It reads the backgrounds painted under its middle line (colors, gradients by their stops, product gradients) and lets the color or the surface that covers most of its width decide. Over a picture or a video, it reads a small thumbnail of the part under the header, overlay included. It reads again as the page scrolls, as a slideshow changes slide and as a Products showcase wipes to the next spotlight.

  • Over a light background, the header keeps its own Color scheme and its main Logo image.
  • Over a dark background, it takes Colors over dark backgrounds, a color scheme with light text (Scheme 2 by default), and swaps in the Logo for dark backgrounds when you upload one. The text color goes to the menu links, the icons, the cart count, the text logo and the focus ring.

An open mega menu, search panel or mobile menu always keeps the standard colors. A floating announcement bar can follow the header with Use the header's colors over dark backgrounds.

Example: white logo and white text over a dark image

  1. In the first section of the page, for example a Hero with a dark image, turn on Allow transparent header.
  2. In the Header section, keep Header behavior on Transparent at top.
  3. Upload the white version of your logo in Logo for dark backgrounds. Your usual logo stays in Logo image.
  4. Set Colors over dark backgrounds to a scheme with white text, such as Scheme 2 of the Balm preset (white on near black).
  5. If a floating announcement bar sits over the same image, tick Use the header's colors over dark backgrounds in the Announcement bar section, so its text turns white with the header.

At the top of the page the logo, the menu and the icons are white over the dark image. On scroll the header takes its card with your usual logo and colors, and the announcement bar returns to its own scheme at the same moment.

Mega menu panels follow the header. With Mega menu surface on Match header, the default, they turn glass over a glass header, and the header card and the open panel form one surface at the opacity you set, so nothing of the page reads through. Under a solid header the panels stay opaque. Set Mega menu surface to Solid to keep opaque panels under a glass header too.

Country and language selectors

Country and language selectors adds them to the header: Both (the default), Country/region only, Language only, or Off. On desktop a globe sits just before the search, account and cart icons, at their size, stroke and colors, the transparent header colors included. Selector style shows the globe alone (Icon only) or followed by the current language and currency codes, such as EN · USD (Icon and code). A click opens a panel in the material of the mega menu: a Country/region list that gives each country's currency, with a search field once there are more than ten countries, and a Language list. The current country and language are checked. The panel hangs under the header, aligned on the globe. While a transparent header sits at rest over the first section, with no background drawn, it opens just under the globe instead, and an open panel follows the header as it materializes on scroll. On phones and tablets the selectors sit at the bottom of the mobile menu, sheet or drawer, as a compact row of buttons that each open their own list. Choosing a country or a language reloads the same page with it.

A selector shows only when there is a choice to make: the country selector needs at least two countries or regions, the language selector at least two languages. A store that sells in one country and one language shows neither, whatever the setting. The Localization block of the footer uses the same form, so both places always offer the same choices.

  1. To sell in more countries, go to Settings, Markets in your Shopify admin and add the countries or regions to a market. Each country shows the currency of its market.
  2. To offer more languages, go to Settings, Languages, add a language, translate your store with a translation app, then publish the language.
  3. Check that each published language is active in the markets where you want to offer it: the selector only lists the languages available to the visitor's market.

Mega menus are built from blocks. Add a Mega menu block and type the exact title of a top level item of your main menu in Linked menu item. Its Layout builds the panel from the sub-links of that item (Links and images, Images only, Links only), or from the Link column, Image, Image cards, Products, Promo and Banner blocks you add to it (Custom blocks). See Mega menu blocks. A top level item without a Mega menu block opens a plain dropdown, each sub-link with the picture of what it points to.

Typical use. A header that floats transparent over the home page hero and turns to glass on scroll, with a mega menu under the Shop item: two link columns and four best sellers.

Footer

WhereFooter group only.

The footer is a row of blocks: Large logo, Brand / logo, Link column (one of your menus), Newsletter, Social media links (the icons of your profiles and the Follow on Shop button), Text and Localization (country or currency, and language selectors, shown only when there is more than one choice). Each block sets its own width and margins for desktop and mobile. The Large logo always takes a row of its own, wherever it sits among the blocks.

Under Typography, Column heading size and Link size each have a desktop and a mobile value (14 and 13 px by default). They are fluid like every text size of the theme: the desktop value applies from 1440 px wide and moves toward the mobile value on narrower screens.

An optional Top decorative band draws a curve, a wave, a diagonal or your own image where the footer meets the section above. The Copyright bar at the bottom can take its own color scheme. Newsletter sign ups are added to your Shopify customers, with the tag you choose.

Typical use. The store name as a large wordmark across the footer, then the logo and tagline, Shop and Help link columns, a newsletter block, and a localization block for a store that sells in several countries.

Block: Large logo

A large logo across the whole footer, on a row of its own. It comes first in the footer the theme ships with, and one footer holds one Large logo.

  • Text, the default, sets the store name, or the Text you type, in the heading font, with the case, weight and style of Theme settings, Typography. The size is worked out so the letters fill the width on one line, and follows the width as the screen changes.
  • Image takes your own artwork. Use an SVG, served as it is, or a transparent PNG at least 3000 px wide, so the logo stays sharp across a large screen; a PNG or JPG is served up to 3840 px wide. Recolor SVG draws a single color SVG in the color you choose; other images keep their own colors. Fill Alt text only when the header logo does not already give the store name: left empty, screen readers skip the image.

Width lines the logo up with the footer columns (Content width) or runs it from one edge of the screen to the other (Full width). Mega menu blocks

A mega menu is a panel that opens under a top level item of the main menu. Add a Mega menu block to the Header section, type the exact title of that menu item in Linked menu item, then add the blocks below inside it. On phones the panel becomes an accordion in the mobile menu.

Mega menu

The panel itself: which menu item opens it, its layout, width, background and padding. Layout builds the panel from the sub-links of the linked menu item (Links and images, Images only or Links only) or from the blocks you add inside it (Custom blocks).

With Custom blocks, the blocks sit on one row of the panel and wrap onto a new row when they do not fit, exactly Block spacing (desktop) apart. Each block has a Width (desktop): Auto (its content), 1/4, 1/3, 1/2, 2/3 or Fill (what the others leave). Horizontal alignment (desktop) and Vertical alignment (desktop) place them when they leave room. Media height (desktop) on Match tallest, the default, gives every block with a picture the height of the tallest one, text under the picture included, so every bottom lines up; Fixed uses the height you set.

In the mobile menu the links come first, then the cards (Promo, Image and each card of Image cards), all of the same width and Card ratio (mobile), laid out by Mobile cards layout: Carousel (one row that scrolls sideways, the next card showing), Grid (two columns) or Stack.
